Question 2bi

  1. Formation: Sedimentary rocks are formed through the deposition, compaction and cementation of sediments, while metamorphic rocks are formed when existing rocks are changed by heat and pressure.
  1. Structure: Sedimentary rocks usually occur in layers or strata, while metamorphic rocks may have a banded, foliated or crystalline structure.
  1. Fossils: Sedimentary rocks may contain fossils of plants and animals, while metamorphic rocks rarely contain fossils because heat and pressure usually destroy them.
  1. Hardness: Sedimentary rocks are generally softer and less compact, while metamorphic rocks are usually harder, denser and more compact.

Question 2bii

  1. Source of Minerals: Rocks provide valuable minerals such as gold, iron, tin and limestone, which are used in industries and manufacturing.

  1. Source of Fuel: Some sedimentary rocks contain fossil fuels such as coal and petroleum, which are important sources of energy for domestic and industrial activities.

Question 4bii

Temperature is measured using a thermometer, usually a maximum and minimum thermometer.

  1. The thermometer is placed in a Stevenson screen to protect it from direct sunlight and rainfall.
  2. The maximum thermometer records the highest temperature, while the minimum thermometer records the lowest temperature within a given period.
  3. The readings are taken from the thermometers at the appropriate observation time.
  4. Temperature is recorded in degrees Celsius (°C).

Question 4biii

Relative humidity is measured using a hygrometer, particularly a wet-and-dry bulb hygrometer (psychrometer).

  1. The instrument consists of two thermometers: a dry-bulb thermometer and a wet-bulb thermometer.
  2. The wet-bulb thermometer is covered with a moist cloth, while the dry-bulb thermometer remains uncovered.
  3. Water evaporating from the wet cloth cools the wet-bulb thermometer, producing a difference between the two thermometer readings.
  4. The difference in readings is used with a relative humidity table to determine the relative humidity, expressed as a percentage (%).

  1. Global Positioning: Global positioning refers to the process of determining the exact location or position of an object or place on the earth's surface using a Global Positioning System (GPS). It uses signals received from satellites to determine the latitude, longitude and altitude of a location.
  1. Attributes: Attributes are descriptive information or characteristics attached to geographical features in a Geographic Information System (GIS). They provide details about features such as the name, size, population, height and type of a particular location or object.

  1. Digitizing: Digitizing is the process of converting geographical information from paper maps, photographs or other analogue sources into digital format for storage, analysis and display in a computer-based GIS. It involves tracing features such as roads, rivers and boundaries.
  1. Map Layer: A map layer is a collection of related geographical information displayed together on a map in a GIS. Each layer represents a particular feature, such as roads, rivers, buildings, vegetation or administrative boundaries.
